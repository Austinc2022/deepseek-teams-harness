# Red-team failure reasons and kill criteria

Use when attacking a shortlisted concept's demand & economics case before it is ranked, recommended or greenlit.

## Steps

1. **Steelman** each candidate in 2-3 lines: the strongest version of the case and the single number it depends on. You cannot red-team a case you have not stated at its best.
2. **Enumerate failure reasons** — one pass per category, at least one candidate failure where plausible: demand (who actually pays, and why now), pricing/WTP (price above evidenced willingness-to-pay), competition/substitution (named incumbent or free alternative), economics (CAC vs contribution, ramp math), channel/reach (how buyers are actually reached), retention/repeat (one-off vs recurring), external/regulatory, team/timing. Use the identify-assumptions-new risk categories to make sure nothing is skipped.
3. **Evidence every failure reason**: record the claim, its source (fact id, file, or named comparable), its strength (measured > simulated > argued > hearsay), and what would falsify it. An evidenced weak objection beats a vivid unsupported one — never invent evidence.
4. **Convert the top failure reasons into kill criteria.** Each kill criterion must be measurable, testable before or shortly after launch, and pre-committed (no moving goalposts): "Kill if <metric> <threshold> by <date / when tested>." At least one per candidate; a candidate with no kill criterion is an unexamined favourite.
5. **Rank**: order by (fewest unresolved kill criteria) → (score per the venture-scoring rubric) → (size of evidenced upside). Output at most 2 with a one-line reason each, and state explicitly which candidates were dropped and why.

## Verify before handing over

- [ ] Every failure reason has evidence + strength + falsifier.
- [ ] Each candidate has ≥1 measurable, pre-committed kill criterion.
- [ ] All candidates attacked with equal force; no favourite left unexamined.
- [ ] Shortlist is ≤2 and has the fewest unresolved kill conditions; dropped candidates named with reasons.

## Mistakes to avoid

Generic objections ("competition may be strong") with no named competitor or number; kill criteria that cannot fail ("keep an eye on churn"); piling onto the weakest candidate; counting the same failure reason twice under two categories.

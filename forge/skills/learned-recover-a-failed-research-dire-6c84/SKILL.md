---
name: learned-recover-a-failed-research-dire-6c84
description: When an agent or team fails a data-gathering directive by exhausting its turn cap on fruitless searches, and you (the supervisor) have direct fetch tools and a deadline to hit.
---

1. Read the failed team's brief and artifacts, not just its final 'failed' line — the brief often contains the exact URLs the agent was told to use, and a teammate may already have succeeded with a leaner method.
2. Identify why it failed specifically: web searches returning 0 results, oversized sitemaps, missing tool permissions. Do not repeat that method.
3. Re-fetch the core source URLs directly yourself in one parallel batch. For store data, extract count, price-as-shown (with region), average and date promptly; do not build a table from memory.
4. Check the artifact directory for a second teammate's result. Even when the supervisor's run 'failed', a capped team may have delivered a full artifact — use it, but independently verify its key rows with your own fetches.
5. Label evidence honestly: counts are measured from the instrument, but a title's classification (e.g. 'generated content') argued from store copy is not measured; 404s and missing slugs are recorded as gaps, not silently filled.
6. Fire the directive's kill condition explicitly in the report if the evidence fails it. Do not soften a negative verdict to make a table look complete.
7. Report to the COO with one evidence line per claim, its source path/URL and strength; record a capability gap on the failed agent and score the teammate who succeeded.
8. Mistakes to avoid: do not open a duplicate ticket when the COO says a persist ticket stays armed; do not edit another agent's tree — a read-only head sends the correction through the COO to the builder; do not keep searching for a 'perfect' comparable after the deadline — deliver with 'NOT FETCHED' gaps.

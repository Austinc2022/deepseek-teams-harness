decision.md structure, in order:
1. Header: concept, claimed segment, verdict (bold, one line), date.
2. Verdict & chosen concept: for stop, state explicitly that no concept/segment is advanced; name the claimed segment so the rejection is traceable.
3. Evidence grading table: one row per signal with strength rank (retained payers > non-refundable > refundable deposits > incentivized waitlist > stated intent), the count, the conversion ratio computed, and what it does/doesn't prove. Unsourced third-party estimates are inadmissible — say so.
4. Score review: recompute the weighted total from stated weights; flag mismatch with stated total; show demand-heavy re-weighting; call out inflated dimensions (novelty/competition) against primary evidence (direct incumbents exist).
5. Revenue arithmetic: observed run rate, realistic range (label conversion assumptions as estimates), stretch; subs needed for $1M/yr and $1M/day; top-of-funnel needed at observed retention; explicit verdict sentence on whether upside reaches the $1M/yr floor and the orders of magnitude vs $1M/day.
6. Reasons tied to scores (one bullet per low score).
7. Conditions for resubmission (pilot design: strangers not giveaway leads, non-refundable, ≥100, 3 months, churn threshold).
8. What would change the verdict (falsifiable).
9. Assumptions to test in the demos (each testable in a demo, each tied to a number in the evidence).
Keep under 1,500 words; every number traceable to evidence pack or shown arithmetic.

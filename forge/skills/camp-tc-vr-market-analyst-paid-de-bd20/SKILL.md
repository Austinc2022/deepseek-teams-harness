description: Use when judging a game or app pitch's demand evidence, especially virtual-pet, casual, free-to-install, paid-DLC, or cross-platform concepts.
---

1. Separate claimed attention from observed payment. For every pitch item, record the exact URL, publication/capture date, platform, price currency, rating count, rating, release date, and what transaction the evidence represents.
2. Verify the named store comparable before reasoning from it. A free demo cannot establish willingness to pay for a premium title, even if its rating is high. A premium comparable from the same creators/team can establish that some buyers pay, but record its price, review count, recency, content scope, and team pedigree rather than treating it as proof for a cheaper add-on model.
3. Prefer official or directly store-sourced snapshots; if using an aggregator, name it, give its retrieval date, and quote what it reports. A page that shows 4.3 stars but no count does not support a count claim. Keep the unverified count explicit.
4. Audit stated-intent evidence (polls, comments, likes, petitions) as attention only. Audit crowdfunding as actual payment only for the specific campaign, reward, date, and platform; do not transfer Quest demand from mobile/AR or backers to units without evidence.
5. Identify regions with actual data. Store language support is not customer geography; reviewer location is not sales location. Record missing per-country units, revenue, conversion, refunds, and price as gaps.
6. Do not convert ratings to sales without a stated, labelled assumption. Sales/revenue estimates must show arithmetic and a range; report ranges as sensitivity cases, not measurements.
7. State which evidence would reverse the decision, and make it findable: comparable SKU/store links, exact price and rating-count snapshots, release dates, campaign URL with funded/backer count and rewards, and available regional sales or country-level review evidence.
8. Common errors: trusting a pitch's rating count, calling free users paying customers, treating upvotes/signatures as orders, inferring region from language, presenting a $9.99 poll as support for a $4.99 pack, or claiming market-wide sales from one title's reviews.

# Demand-gate IC verdict procedure

1. **Isolate verified paying demand.** Count only money moved by named buyers in the target segment. Quarantine: free waitlists, top-down market forecasts, grant-subsidised portions (count the buyer's own-budget share separately), unsigned quotes, contingent POs (list separately with the contingency).
2. **Read the fixture/call logs, not just the summary.** Buyers state their conversion conditions verbatim (e.g. "need 30% error-drop evidence", "subject to acceptance test"); those become your must-fix conditions and demo-stage assumptions.
3. **Do the revenue math from observed price points only.** Use real quoted prices (pilot fee, per-seat annual). Estimate addressable logo count with `(estimate: method)` and the arithmetic shown. Produce y1PerDay, y3PerDay, ceilingPerDay plus annual figures; compare explicitly against the gate ($1M/yr) and the aspiration ($1M/day = $365M/yr). Never inflate to hit the aspiration; say plainly when the aspiration is unreachable.
4. **Apply the gate as written.** Go is allowed only if (a) verified paying demand from a named segment AND (b) realistic upside reaches $1M/year. Necessary, not sufficient: also check kill criteria and whether near-term revenue is contingent on the risky build items (if the acceptance test is exactly the feasibility risk, that is a condition, not a kill).
5. **Calibrate verdict.** go = both gate legs pass with manageble conditions; revise-once = a material but fixable evidence/plan gap; stop = a gate leg fails or a kill criterion fires. Score with the shared rubric; label estimated dimensions.
6. **Write decision.md**: verdict; concept+segment; reasons tied to given scores; conditions (numbered, testable, with failure consequences); revenue range with own math vs both thresholds; what would change the verdict (both directions); demo-stage assumptions; score + must-fix + evidence refs.
7. **Common mistakes**: counting waitlists or TAM forecasts as demand; ignoring grant funding in pilot payments; treating quote requests as orders; averaging away a 3-buyer concentration risk; letting the prior analyst memo's numbers leak into the math.

## Verification
- Every revenue number traces to a price in evidence or an `estimate:` with arithmetic shown.
- Final verdict states, in one line each, the honest answer vs $1M/yr and vs $1M/day.

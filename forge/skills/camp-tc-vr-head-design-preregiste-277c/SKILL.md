## Procedure
1. Turn every risky claim into a falsifiable hypothesis before any build or test. Specify device/software, participants, task, timing, sampling, scoring, and exact numeric pass/fail rules.
2. Separate outcome families: performance (real presented frames plus frame-time percentiles and hitching rate), comprehension (observable unprompted completion), comfort (validated symptom scale plus abort rules), and fun (repeat/choice/voluntary-continuation behavior). Do not convert a post-test 1–5 'fun?' average into proof of fun.
3. Allocate one demo turn per genuinely unresolved high-consequence risk. Re-test an earlier prototype when criteria were written afterward; retire a demo only when comparable evidence already exists. Fold a lower-risk portability check into a funded demo when it needs no special prototype and cannot alter the core design decision.
4. For comfort tests, run seated in a standardized environment; collect baseline, interim, post, and delayed symptoms; set stop rules in advance. An optional comfort toggle is not a substitute for testing the actual locomotion configuration.
5. Treat failure in comfort, safety, or required hardware as a veto regardless of performance or fun. Record all results, including missing/inconclusive evidence, in a fixed evaluation table.
6. Test causal-feeling systems with measurable event-to-output latency and a blind identification task, not preference questions. Use signal-processing or platform tools for audio latency and randomized stimuli/control pulses.
7. Preserve negative decisions and the evidence that would reopen them. Mark launch risks that cannot be closed in sandbox or early demo environments as explicit residual risks.
8. For publication plans, cite current first-party regional and store requirements; verify WebXR/browser distribution separately rather than assuming browser delivery equals store distribution.

## Checks and common mistakes
- Was the criterion fixed before the test, or rationalized afterward?
- Did the performance metric measure actual presented frames, not only a software update-rate counter?
- Are percentages and thresholds defined against a fixed denominator?
- Is 'fun' observable behavior rather than a self-report rating?
- Can discomfort abort the run even when performance and enjoyment look good?
- Does each funded demo isolate the feature whose risk it is meant to resolve?
- Are failed/unrun checks kept visible rather than silently treated as passes?

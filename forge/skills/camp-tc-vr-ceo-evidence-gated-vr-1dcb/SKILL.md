description: When demo evaluations arrive and you must decide go / revise demos / stop and fix the final scope, cut list, budget and revenue framing for a VR title.
---

1. Do the revenue arithmetic first, before reading any pitch: target ÷ price = copies/day for BOTH the per-day and per-year targets (e.g. $1M/day ÷ $20 = 50,000/day; $1M/year ÷ $20 ÷ 365 ≈ 137/day). Write these numbers into the decision so no one hand-waves them.
2. Build a per-demo scorecard of measured behaviour only: voluntary repeat play (≥N runs), unprompted replay intent, completion, mean session length, fun rating, framerate vs target, comfort incidents. Classify every claim in memos as MEASURED / ADJECTIVE / HYPOTHESIS. Only MEASURED counts as fun evidence.
3. A feature enters the final list only with measured repeat-play evidence at the scope proposed. If evidence supports only a smaller version (e.g. a 30-60s step, not a mode), downscope it and attach a numeric kill criterion evaluated at a milestone gate.
4. Cut any feature with zero unprompted replay intent, regardless of who champions it. Treat framerate below the target as an automatic fail for that feature (it is also a comfort risk). Stakeholder praise (art director "visually impressive", producer "viral hook") never overrides measured behaviour: virality hypotheses require replay/share intent data.
5. Cut list items get numeric re-entry conditions (must pass a fresh re-demo with stated thresholds) so "cut" is falsifiable, not political.
6. Effort budget: team size × weeks, and state what the cuts made possible (e.g. cutting multiplayer is what keeps the budget honest). Milestone gates must replicate the original demo protocol with fresh testers; add a separate demand-validation gate (wishlists/conversion) because fun metrics cannot set marketing spend.
7. Restate revenue honestly: say explicitly what the demos do and do not support (fun/retention quality ≠ demand/sales volume). If the headline target needs volume no demo can evidence, say the go covers building the fun only, and if the headline target is a hard requirement, that alone justifies stop.
Common mistakes: letting one enthusiast adjective override data; counting prompted survey intent as unprompted replay; keeping a failing "hook" feature alive on hypothesis; promising revenue from fun metrics; skipping the copies/day arithmetic.

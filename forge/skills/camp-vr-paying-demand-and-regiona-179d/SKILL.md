# Paying-demand evidence and regional split

Use when evidencing that a customer segment will pay, or when building the regional split for a demand report.

## Steps

1. **Define the segment observably.** Who they are, what device they own, what they have already bought. No invented personas: every segment must map to evidence you can cite.

2. **Paying-demand evidence ladder — strongest first:**
   1. Unit/revenue estimates for paid comparables (third-party trackers such as SteamSpy, VG Insights, Gamalytic or App Lab stats) with URL + access date.
   2. Price points at which comparable titles sell at scale (review counts at that price tier).
   3. Owner/review counts as a sales proxy: state the ratio you use and cite its source; label the result an estimate, never a measurement.
   4. Chart presence and how long a comparable held rank.
   5. Interest signals (wishlists, followers, upvotes): label these as interest only — they are never paying evidence.

3. **Regional split.** Derive shares from signals you can source: regional review-language shares, storefront chart regions, regional pricing, platform hardware/regional stats. Each share names its derivation; percentages sum to 100. If only global numbers exist, mark the split as reasoned and add the split to the unverified-claims ledger.

4. **Verify before handover:**
   - [ ] Each segment line has at least one paying-evidence citation (URL + date)
   - [ ] No interest signal is counted as paying demand
   - [ ] Every derived number names its base number and multiplier/ratio
   - [ ] Regional split sums to 100% with derivations named
   - [ ] Anything unsourced moved to the unverified-claims ledger, not asserted

## Common mistakes

- Equating wishlist/follower counts with paying demand.
- Review-to-sales ratios used without stating the ratio or its source.
- A regional split invented to fill the field instead of derived from signals.
- Segments named by demographics alone with no behavioral evidence.

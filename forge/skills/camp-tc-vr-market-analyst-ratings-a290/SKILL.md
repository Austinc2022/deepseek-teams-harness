---
name: camp-tc-vr-market-analyst-ratings-a290
description: A pitch deck or client claims a game's revenue "from store ratings/reviews" and you must verify whether the figure survives before it is repeated.
---

1. Pull the observed record: rating/review counts per store, list price, release date, price/promo history, regional prices. Note capture date vs. the claimed period (counts may include months outside the window).
2. Convert ratings to units at BOTH ends of your house conversion bands (e.g. Quest 25-80 sales/rating, Steam 30-90 per review), per store, never merged. Units = ratings × band.
3. Estimate realised price, not list: weight promo periods and regional price mix; expect $1-3.50 below US list at this price tier. State the assumption.
4. Gross range = low units × low realised price to high units × high realised price; net = gross × 0.70. Label both "estimate".
5. Run the ceiling test: max units (highest conversion) × list price with zero discounting. If the ceiling is still below the claimed figure, the claim fails on arithmetic alone — lead with that.
6. If the ceiling passes, solve for the implied conversion (units needed / ratings) and compare to the band. Check price history: promos push realised price down, so they can never rescue a claim that needs higher realised price.
7. Treat method-free third-party figures (undated blogs) as unverified; check whether their implied conversion is even inside your band before citing them.
8. Verdict = one sentence: repeatable as stated / not repeatable. Settling datum = one reported unit-sales or gross-receipts number from the publisher or store.
Mistakes to avoid: multiplying ratings by both ends of a band together; using list price as realised price; letting a post-window promo or ratings drift inflate the first-year window silently; quoting a blog's unit figure as fact.

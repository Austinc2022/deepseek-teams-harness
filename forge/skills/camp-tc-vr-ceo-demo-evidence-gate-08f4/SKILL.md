description: When deciding go/revise/stop from VR demo evaluations and fixing scope, cuts, budget and revenue expectations.
---

1. Sort every demo claim into measured behaviour (repeat play, completion, session length, ratings, fps, nausea counts) vs adjective/opinion (one enthusiast's taste: "visually impressive", "relaxing", producer "viral hook" claims). Only measured behaviour admits features.
2. Check each demo against hard studio bars first: fps target (e.g. 72), comfort incidence. A demo under the fps bar or with majority discomfort is cut regardless of praise.
3. Admit a feature only if it has its own measured evidence (replay/completion/session/rating). If a needed feature (e.g. economy scaffolding) has no evidence, admit it explicitly as scaffolding with a budget cap and a gate, never launder it as "validated".
4. Management pressure (producer memos, art director quotes) that contradicts measurement gets named and rejected explicitly, citing the contradicting numbers (e.g. 0/8 replay vs "viral hook").
5. Do revenue arithmetic before promising anything: copies/day = target/price. Compare to installed base for the big number; separate retention evidence (replay intent) from demand evidence (wishlist, willingness-to-pay). Testers playing free prove zero about sales. State median/upside honestly and name the missing evidence (store conversion, paid funnel).
6. Output: verdict (go/revise/stop, scoped); feature list each with its evidence; cut list each with its failing numbers; effort budget in person-weeks with milestone gates and a kill gate (stop if external playtest falls below demo thresholds); revenue against both targets. Note what demos do NOT support.

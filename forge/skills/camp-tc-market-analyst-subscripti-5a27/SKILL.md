name: camp-tc-market-analyst-subscripti-5a27
description: When asked whether a subscription product can hit a daily or annual revenue target, or to compare a proposed target against benchmark subscriber counts.
---

1. Convert every target to one unit: daily gross revenue. (Annual target / 365.)
2. Fix assumptions explicitly: monthly price, monthly churn, annual revenue per subscriber = price x 12 (gross, while retained); note platform fee separately as a haircut (Meta Quest store ~25%).
3. Compute subscribers needed = targetPerDay / (price x 12 / 365). This is the single most useful number.
4. Express that subscriber count three ways: (a) as a multiple of the best-performing competitor's paying subs, (b) as a % of the platform's total MAU, (c) as steady-state replacement signups per month = subs x monthly churn.
5. Benchmark against realistic ceiling: the best app in category is the de facto ceiling for a new entrant with small budget; anything requiring >3-5x the category leader is not plausible, say so plainly.
6. For the "floor" target (often ~$1M/year = $2,740/day), repeat the same arithmetic; a floor needing <10% of the category leader's subscriber base is usually reachable, state that plainly too.
7. Give a realistic annual revenue range as subscriber range x price x 12, and label the tiers (indie / venture / category leader) instead of one point estimate.
8. Never use top-down TAM share ("1% of a $T market") as a forecast; if a TAM is mentioned, convert it to paying users and show it exceeds the platform's MAU.
9. State plainly: which targets miss and by what multiple, and what would have to be true (price, MAU growth, conversion) for the miss to close. No hedging words ("could potentially").

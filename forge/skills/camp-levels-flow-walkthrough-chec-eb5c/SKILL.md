Use this before delivering levels.md: validate the player experience by simulating the path beat by beat instead of trusting the outline.

1. **Walk the path in order.** Read the level top to bottom and simulate the player: at each beat write one line — what the player sees, does, and should feel. If you cannot say what the player does at a beat, it is not designed yet; mark it INCOMPLETE.
2. **Build the mechanic teaching ledger.** List every mechanic the game introduces. For each, mark its beats in the level order as INTRO (safe, no fail state, teaches the verb), PRACTICE (low stakes), TEST (meaningful challenge), MASTERY/COMBINATION (optional). A mechanic used in a challenge before its INTRO beat is a hard defect — reorder or add the teaching beat.
3. **Check the pacing rhythm.** Alternate tension and release: no more than ~2–3 high-intensity encounters without a rest/breather beat; place a checkpoint or safe space immediately after each spike. Confirm first-level intensity is the lowest and the curve rises without an unexplained cliff between levels.
4. **Hunt for flow defects.** Flag: dead ends with no reward or purpose; backtracking without a new reason (opened shortcut, new ability); beats that spoil a later surprise via sightline or audio; encounters where the player can leave the play space or skip the teaching moment; pacing words that contradict the content ("quiet exploration" followed immediately by a boss).
5. **Cross-check prerequisites.** Nothing in level N may require an item, ability or knowledge defined in level N+1 or left undefined anywhere.

**Verify before handover:** every mechanic has INTRO → PRACTICE → TEST in order; every beat has a player action; pacing alternates and has rest after spikes; zero dead ends, skips or prerequisite violations; each level states explicitly what teaches each new mechanic. Common mistakes: assuming the reader will infer the teaching beat, listing encounters as a set-piece list with no order, and forgetting the player's first 60 seconds in a level.

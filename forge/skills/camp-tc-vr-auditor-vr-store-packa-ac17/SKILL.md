# Store package audit procedure

1. Read the plan, the package, and the product fact sheet yourself — never trust the team's summary claims.
2. Build a coverage matrix: every required region and language must appear in the package. Any omission defeats any "complete" claim.
3. Check every claim in the listing copy against the build facts (feature counts, multiplayer, reviews, ratings). Fabricated claims (e.g. review counts, co-op) are instant must-fix: they are false advertising and store-policy violations.
4. Check pricing against the plan's rules: draft marking, per-region reasoning tied to evaluation data, owner approval of finals. "Marked FINAL without owner approval" and generic benchmark reasoning are defects. Cross-check price level direction vs. evaluation signals (e.g. price-sensitive regions priced high).
5. Check compliance: ratings (per region, not just one territory), health/safety, privacy, refund text; every machine-translated string must carry a native-review flag. "Copied from another game" privacy text and "no legal issues" claims are unsupported without lawyer review evidence.
6. Charter check: enumerate anything that is submission, publishing, spend, or outreach. Emails to press/streamers, filings with ratings bodies, and reserved ad spend are breaches or near-breaches even when described as "prep". The words "nothing was submitted" beside a filing or sent emails are a contradiction — flag it explicitly.
7. Evidence gaps: list what proof is missing for each claim (native-speaker sign-off records, competitor price data, ratings certificate, lawyer review, owner authorizations).
8. Verdict: reject if any fabricated copy claim, charter breach, or required region/language is missing. Separate the incident escalation (owner must know about unauthorized outreach immediately) from the remediation package. List owner-only actions explicitly.

Common mistakes to avoid: accepting "machine translated + a friend glanced" as native review; treating "reserved" ad credit as harmless prep; forgetting non-US ratings bodies; letting "complete, ready to submit" stand when a region is missing.

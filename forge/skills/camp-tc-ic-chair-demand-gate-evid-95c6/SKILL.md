description: When judging a paid-demand pilot at the demand gate (deposits, refunds, retained payers, waitlists, survey intent).
---

1. Grade every demand signal by strength: retained payers (strongest) > non-refundable payments > refundable deposits > incentivized waitlist > stated survey intent (weakest). Never pool them into one "demand" number.
2. Compute the funnel ratios: refunds/deposits, keepers/deposits, month-N actives/keepers AND month-N actives/deposits. Report the actives/depositors rate — it is the honest conversion for scaling.
3. Revenue arithmetic: ARPU/yr = monthly price × 12. Subs needed for $1M/yr = 1,000,000/ARPU; for $1M/day = 365,000,000/ARPU. Then required top-of-funnel = needed subs / observed actives-per-depositor rate.
4. Give a range, not one number: observed run rate (actives × price), realistic (waitlist × 1-5% for incentivized leads; stated intent × 5-15% for surveys), stretch. Convert each to $/day for tier comparison.
5. Check the verdict weights: recompute the weighted score yourself; stated weights often don't reproduce the stated total. Weights that favor novelty/competition over demand inflate verdicts — re-weight demand ≥ 50% and see if the decision flips.
6. Cross-check the claimed segment against the survey population (e.g., gym-goers surveyed for an at-home product = mismatch). Exclude unsourced blog estimates from evidence.
7. Answer explicitly: does any honest upside reach the $1M/yr floor? If no, say so and name the orders of magnitude vs $1M/day.
8. Verdict discipline: adverse retained-payer churn (majority of committed payers gone by month 3) is a negative signal, not thin-but-neutral. Require a non-refundable pilot with ≥100 strangers before revisiting.

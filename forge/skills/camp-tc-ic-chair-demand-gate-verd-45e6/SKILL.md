description: When making a go / revise-once / stop demand-gate decision from a scorecard fixture with demand scores and an analyst revenue projection.
---

1. Read the fixture scorecard and the analyst memo; extract every claim that carries a number.
2. Re-do the analyst's arithmetic yourself before trusting any projection. Common failure: TAM quoted as reachable revenue (100% penetration), and annual revenue confused with per-day (e.g. $14.4M/yr is $39k/day, not $1M/day).
3. Run the license-model math: groups × price = annual; annual/365 = per-day. Compute groups needed for $1M/day and $1M/year and compare to the total addressable universe and to external benchmarks (e.g. HR-tech y3 adoption 60-100 groups).
4. Score the five fixture dimensions (buyer pain, verified paying demand, evidence quality, delivery readiness, revenue credibility) 0-5 each; weight paying demand highest, then delivery readiness; convert to a 0-100 gate score (66-74 = revise-once band is typical when demand is real but delivery/projection are weak).
5. Pick the verdict: go only if paying demand is verified AND delivery readiness is not a blocker AND the projection survives arithmetic; stop if demand is unverified/verbal-only or the model cannot reach even the smaller target; otherwise revise-once.
6. Tie each reason to a named score. State revenue three ways: realistic y1, realistic y3 (benchmark-based), best-case ceiling. Compare explicitly against both $1M/day and $1M/year.
7. Attach conditions that directly fix the weak scores (e.g. convert verbal commits, solve the delivery objection that lost a deal). List what flips the verdict both ways and assumptions for demos.
8. Watch for evidence-quality traps: verbal commitments counted as customers, single-cycle renewals treated as retention. Flag them in evidence quality reasons.

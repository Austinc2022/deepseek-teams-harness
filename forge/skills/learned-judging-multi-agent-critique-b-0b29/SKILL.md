---
name: learned-judging-multi-agent-critique-b-0b29
description: When scoring a fast pressure-test / second-opinion deliverable that is split into per-author sections (design, art, etc.) answering a fixed item list.
---

1. Per author, check every requested item is answered in the form asked (named prior art + why it threatens, a verdict with a failure mode, one concrete fix).
2. Spot-check recalled references: are the named titles real and mechanically the closest threat? Penalize vague substitutes ("a mobile puzzle game") but not a defensible near-miss.
3. Hunt contradictions between parallel agents on the same judgment (e.g. "most VR-native" vs "only genuinely native verb"). An unflagged, unreconciled contradiction is a method gap: second opinions must say where they disagree.
4. Check the request's framing constraints (build budget, lines, platform, word cap) are actually used in the analysis, not just restated. Apply format limits to the COMBINED deliverable, not each agent's slice.
5. Score evidence discipline separately: argued/recall claims labeled as such and deferred to the measured source is correct when the requester owns evidence collection; two agents repeating a recall claim is still argued, not measured.
6. Score against the best such brief in the field: sharp failure modes with mitigations and time-stamped beats beat longer surveys. Do not reward volume or confidence.
Common mistake: treating convergence between agents as verification; it is correlated recall, not evidence.

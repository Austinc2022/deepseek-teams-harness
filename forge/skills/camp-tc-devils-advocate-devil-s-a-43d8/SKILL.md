description: Reviewing a demand/economics report where totals may be arithmetically right but inputs are optimistic — before approving or killing a candidate.
---

1. Recompute every multiplication; label each "arithmetic right / input fantasy" and quantify the fantasy (e.g., realised price vs list).
2. Convert revenue goals into the unit the business actually sells (sites, seats, headcount), using realised not list price where pilot evidence exists.
3. Put plan headcount side-by-side with headcount the goal requires at the report's own close-rate assumption; compute years-to-goal at the plan's own production rate.
4. Treat LOIs as options, not traction: apply the observed LOI→paid conversion to the pipeline; check what paying customers actually paid and its effect on per-unit revenue.
5. Check every cost assumption (e.g., "customer pays hardware") for evidence; if unsubstantiated, compute the drag if the vendor pays.
6. Verify scores trace to evidence; flag scores justified by n=2 or discounted buyers.
7. Check segment/region splits exist with evidence quality; undisclosed splits = hidden TAM risk.
8. State plainly which goal is realistic, give a defensible revenue range, and set kill criteria with numeric thresholds measurable in 2 quarters.

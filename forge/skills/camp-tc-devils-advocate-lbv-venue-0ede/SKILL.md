# Reviewing a physical venue / LBE candidate (LBVR, arcades, franchised attractions)

1. **Decode the launch datapoint into capacity units.** Revenue ÷ ticket price = players; players ÷ days = players/day. Then compute venue capacity: stations × (open hours ÷ session length) = session-slots/day; × players/session = player-slots/day. Report launch demand as % of player-slots. If ≥90%, demand is *censored* by supply — you learned nothing about true demand, only that supply was short. Never extrapolate a sold-out weekend.

2. **List launch-weekend contamination** explicitly: opening PR/press, novelty/first-visit, friends-&-family/comps/promo pricing, weekend-only (no weekday signal), pent-up local curiosity, single best-case metro, weather/season/event, no repeat behaviour yet. State that the least representative week cannot be the model.

3. **Build steady state from capacity, not from the launch number.** Assign day-of-week utilisation (Mon-Thu trough is decisive: 15-35%; Fri 40-65%; Sat 65-95%; Sun 45-75%), a realistic players/session (launch group bookings inflate this; solo walk-ins pull toward 1.5-1.7). Show low/base/high cases with the full arithmetic so it can be argued with. Cross-check against a real comparable (e.g., Sandbox VR FDD ~$1.7M/venue/yr).

4. **Test margin claims at steady-state volume, not launch volume.** Recompute the claimed contribution margin with (a) only the costs the report lists, (b) the realistic full stack the report omits: marketing, insurance, utilities, payment processing, consumables, software, equipment amortisation/refresh (tech life 4-5 yrs), franchise royalty (industry norm 5% franchise + 10-20% content/experience on gross). Find the revenue floor below which the claimed margin dies: fixed ÷ (margin target − variable% − royalty%).

5. **Scale arithmetic.** Goal/year ÷ venue revenue = venues. Goal/day × 365 likewise. Capital = venues × build cost (flag if the build cost is far below/above a known FDD comparable). Rollout = venues ÷ openings/month (LBE franchisors realistically 2-4/month), + 6-12 months ramp per venue. Compare the required system to real chains for sanity (Dave & Buster's ~243 venues ≈ $6-7M/day; Sandbox VR ~80 venues ≈ $0.27M/day after a decade).

6. **Demand per segment and region with evidence quality labels.** For each segment (groups/corporate, teens/families, tourists, enthusiasts) and region type (best-case metro vs suburbs vs secondary vs tourist strip): what pays, what evidence, quality rating (FDD/filing > multi-venue operator data > single venue steady state > single launch weekend = anecdotal). Flag weekday-vs-weekend segment mismatch (corporate demand is weekday; launch data is weekend).

7. **Write kill criteria that a venue can actually trip:** sustained rolling-13-week thresholds after a 90-day honeymoon (utilisation, weekday utilisation, contribution margin on full stack, repeat rate within 90 days, players/session), plus a system-level criterion (e.g., fewer than X of first 10 franchise venues above $Y annualised by month 18 kills the scale plan). Reject unmeasurable criteria like "if a venue underperforms, close it".

8. **Verdict discipline:** judge each revenue goal separately (per-year vs per-day); give realistic ranges (venue-level $/day and system-level $/day) and state plausibility plainly. A goal can be arithmetically reachable and still implausible on rollout, capital and sector track record — say both.

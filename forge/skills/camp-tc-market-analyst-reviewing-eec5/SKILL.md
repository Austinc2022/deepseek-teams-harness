---
name: camp-tc-market-analyst-reviewing-eec5
description: When asked to review another team's demand-discovery / market report against explicit acceptance criteria before a greenlight meeting.
---

1. Read the report once end-to-end, then re-read with the acceptance criteria in hand; never let a defect in through a paraphrase — every verdict must quote exact report text plus its section.
2. Check citations at the source-list level, not just in-text: a citation only counts if it has BOTH a URL and a date. Watch for reserved/placeholder domains (e.g. `.example`, `localhost`) — a URL that cannot resolve is not a verifiable URL.
3. For revenue figures, verify two separate things: (a) a stated method, (b) a consistent estimate-vs-observed label. A line reading "estimated ... (observed)" is a contradiction; a source marked "no method" fails the method test. Check the report's own claims about its data (e.g. "we used those in the revenue lines") actually appear in the body.
4. Distinguish payment evidence (comparable paid titles, price points, review counts, regions) from interest evidence (surveys asking "would you", wishlist adds, downloads of free titles, MAU). Interest never satisfies a willingness-to-pay criterion. Named adjacent titles are not a customer segment.
5. Screen the portfolio for the non-obvious-gap criterion: if every candidate is "adjacent to" a chart topper, the criterion fails regardless of how well each candidate is argued.
6. Check process criteria literally: emails to external companies are outreach even when framed as research; a partial-response sample (e.g. 6 of 14) is self-selection-biased.
7. Rank the three most damaging defects by which greenlight decision each distorts (process integrity, demand truth, valuation anchor) — not by how easy they are to fix. Cosmetic or bookkeeping defects rank below ones that would corrupt a decision.
8. Choose reject over revise when the minimum fixes require new research (new candidates, new payment evidence) rather than edits to existing text.
9. Close with an overall verdict and a minimum fix list that maps 1:1 to the failed criteria.

description: When judging a monetisation sketch for a premium (buy-to-own) game and writing a monetisation plan with revenue arithmetic.
---

1. Inventory the evidence fixture separately from the sketch's claims; mark each sketch claim as supported / assumed / contradicted.
2. Set price from WTP evidence: compute expected gross per respondent at each price point (share x price) before trusting any single tier; note self-selection bias direction (overstates WTP).
3. Regional pricing: never straight FX. Use PPP reasoning, reference Steam's region groups (LATAM-USD/MENA-USD since Nov 2023 - daily-rate conversion is impossible there) and Steamworks' PPP/multi-variable conversion. Give 3+ regions with PPP percentages of US price and local psychological endings.
4. Judge every purchase item against the dark-pattern rule: reject paid randomization (loot boxes), expiring paid benefits, fake scarcity, streak resets, paid skips of the core win. Revise open-ended 'all future content' promises (unpriced liability) into bounded editions.
5. Revenue math: net/unit = price x (1 - store cut, ~30% on Quest/Steam). Build low/base/high from comparable category launch-month units (e.g. paid VR puzzle 10k-30k), cross-check with wishlist count x 5-15% conversion. Decay m2 ~35%, m3 ~18% of m1 unless data says otherwise.
6. Teardown headline goals explicitly: compare required units/day against category best-launch-month, and against the category's #1 title lifetime average (e.g. Beat Saber $255M / ~5 yrs). Point out where a DAU x daily-spend formula wrongly applies subscription logic to buy-to-own products.
7. End with missing-evidence list and state how each moves the numbers (direction and magnitude).
8. Keep the doc under the word cap: count with wc-style tokens, convert tables to compact lines, cut redundancy between sections last.

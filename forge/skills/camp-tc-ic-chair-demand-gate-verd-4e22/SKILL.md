description: When chairing a demand-gate / investment-committee decision where evidence is surveys, free demos, LOIs, and vendor TAM slides.
---

1. Classify each evidence item: survey self-report = stated intent (upper bound only); free-demo enthusiasm = zero (nothing paid); verbal WTP on a leading question = zero; conditional LOIs ("if grant pays") = zero; vendor top-down TAM = ignore unless denominator and price are sourced. 2. Cross-check survey magnitudes against field notes; a single practitioner's observed case count often contradicts aggregated survey claims by an order of magnitude — treat the survey as inflated. 3. Do bottom-up math: revenue floor ÷ price = units needed (e.g., $1M/yr ÷ $15 = 66,667 sessions/yr = 183/day; ÷ $1,200 site price = 834 paying sites). Compare to plausible adoption share of the named segment. Compare $1M/day separately ($365M/yr) and label it aspiration. 4. Identify the actual payer and ask whether THAT payer said they would pay (a dentist saying "maybe if it kept one case and price was right" is a conditional ROI anchor, not demand). 5. Verdict: go only if verified paying demand exists AND realistic (not stretch) upside clears the $1M/yr floor. revise-once if one falsifiable revision (paid pilots + efficacy proof) could clear the floor. stop if the niche ceiling or payer refusal structurally keeps realistic upside below the floor. 6. Always write: revenue range with own math, conditions with numeric thresholds (e.g., "≥5 practices pay ≥$500 from operating budget, no grants"), what-would-change-the-verdict, and demo-stage assumptions. Mistakes to avoid: averaging unverified evidence into a score; letting a fake TAM slide set the range; granting go on intent; letting a conditional LOI count as commitment; two revisions when the first one's conditions failed.

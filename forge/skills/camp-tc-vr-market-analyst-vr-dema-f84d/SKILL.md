description: When building a demand/disvenue candidate report for a VR store (Meta Quest etc.) from an internal store snapshot, review-theme scrape, and junior notes.
---

1. Fix the evidence tiers first: internal store snapshot = observed record (prices, rating counts, release dates, badge strings, regional price capture); internal review scrape = observed complaint counts (state N, date, counting rule); web claims only with URL + retrieval date; everything else → unverified section. 2. Convert ratings to units ONLY with the studio house range (1 rating per 25–80 paid sales, internal claim file); show every multiplication; label each figure ESTIMATE or OBSERVED; give low/central/high (low = ratings×25 at low attach, high = ratings×80 at high attach, central = midpoint ratings × 52.5). 3. Anchor each candidate's revenue on a comparable's observed rating count × a stated capture assumption (e.g. 10–25% of the incumbent's rating base for a challenger, 40–100% where the comp has loud product gaps); never invent a multiplier without a source (the "ratings × price × 20" trick is a trap). 4. Segment = paying evidence only: what was bought (title), price, rating count, and which regions appear in the snapshot; complaints from purchase-verified reviews count as paying evidence; upvotes, petitions, and stated intent go to unverified. 5. Regional split: if the snapshot has only list prices/badges for a few titles, say the revenue split is unsupported and list needed data (per-storefront ratings, per-country units, regional discount history); give launch-region plan separately, labelled plan not observation. 6. Ensure ≥1 candidate is a gap (fixes a top complaint cluster or serves a thin/absent segment), not a chart copy; name which is which. 7. Unverified section must include all junior/intern notes (no URLs, guessed multipliers, unlocatable quotes) and any web claim you couldn't fetch. 8. Verify all arithmetic twice before writing; cite comp store pages as URL + snapshot capture date.
